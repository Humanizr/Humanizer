# Dates, times, durations, and age

## Orientation

Choose the operation by meaning:

- Humanize `DateTime` or `DateTimeOffset` for a relative phrase such as
  “yesterday.” Humanizer `2.11.10` and later also support `DateOnly` and
  `TimeOnly` on compatible target frameworks.
- Humanize `TimeSpan` for elapsed duration such as “2 hours, 5 minutes.”
- In `3.0.1` and later, call `ToAge` on a `TimeSpan` for an age phrase such as
  “two years old.”

## Example

Inject the comparison instant and culture instead of reading the clock inside an example or test:

<CodeBlock language="csharp" title="Program.cs">{dates}</CodeBlock>

The program deterministically prints `yesterday; 2 hours, 5 minutes`.

`precision` controls how many non-empty duration units are returned. `maxUnit` and `minUnit` constrain which units may appear.

Humanizer `3.0.1` and later can render an age with
`TimeSpan.FromDays(750).ToAge(culture, toWords: true)`. It returns
`two years old` for the culture used above.

## Pitfall

`TimeSpan` has no calendar context. Humanizer therefore approximates months and years when decomposing a duration. Use calendar arithmetic on `DateOnly` or `DateTime` when month length, leap years, or birthdays must be exact.

For `DateTime`, keep the input and comparison basis consistent: explicitly choose UTC or local behavior and inject `dateToCompareAgainst`.

## Version notes

`DateOnly` and `TimeOnly` humanization first appears in `2.11.10` and requires a
compatible target framework. `ToAge` first appears in `3.0.1`. The shared
`DateTime`, `DateTimeOffset`, and `TimeSpan` calls shown here run across the
supported corpus.
